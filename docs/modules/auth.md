# auth · who you are, what you may do, what you may see

- **What it does:** `POST /v1/auth/login` sets an httpOnly, SameSite=Strict cookie holding only a signed user id. Every request reloads the role and scope from `users` (`app/core/auth.py`). The local listener has a password-less "sign in as…" picker (DEMO_MODE only) listing **three profiles** from `demo.sign_in_picker` in the config: Duty officer (`rdas`), Rescue team (`boat5`), Demo admin (`admin`). The other 9 seeded users are off the picker but real (the permission and IDOR tests sign in as them; D22). The public listener always needs a password.
- **Key tables / files:** `users` (role, incident_ids, district_ids, team_id), `config/roles.yaml` (action → roles), `assignments` (a team lead's live cases).
- **The tricky rule:** permissions are two separate checks. `require("action")` says whether your role may do something; the `Scope` decides which rows you can see, and it goes **inside the SQL WHERE** of every repository query (rule R8), never a filter applied afterwards. The WebSocket and the audit list use the same Scope.
- **Visible denial:** a team lead opening a case its team doesn't hold gets **403 "Not assigned to your team"**; anyone else out of scope gets a 404 (no hint that the case exists).
- **How to show it:** sign in as R. Das (Gopalganj) and as M. Kumar (Muzaffarpur): different queues from the same table. Mutations must be JSON (415 otherwise), which with SameSite=Strict closes CSRF.
