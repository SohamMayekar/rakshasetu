# location · landmark words → a pin with an honest circle

- **What it does:** `app/modules/location/ladder.py` turns the extracted words ("Thawe gaon, Durga mandir ke peeche, pul ke paas") into a pin, a radius and a **method sentence** ("High · 150 m · matched Thawe durga mata mandir + bridge (unclassified road), 120 m apart, in Thawe"), using the committed OSM gazetteer — and, only when that cannot hold the place, the free OSM geocoders (Photon → Nominatim), cached and named on the pin. No paid service is ever called.
- **Key tables:** `places`, `landmarks`, `districts.boundary` (all from `data/scenarios/BR/gazetteer`).
- **The ladder:** village match (any script, fuzzy ≥ 85, trailing qualifiers dropped when the whole name matches nothing; or a gazetteer scan of the text) → landmarks of each mentioned type within 2 km → two types: closest pair ≤ 500 m → midpoint, 150 m (High only if the village matched and the pair is unique within 1 km, or a landmark matched by name) → one type: the single feature (300 m, Medium) or a tight cluster (centroid, ≤ 1 km), never an average of candidates > 500 m apart → village only (1.5 km, Low, dashed) → district only (15 km, Very low, needs location) → nothing (no pin, "Needs location"; never dropped). A landmark the caller **named** ("DMart in Powai") takes the nearest feature with that name to the stated place; a name matching exactly **one** mapped feature anywhere in the city pins it too — 300 m, Medium, with its district; a name the city holds many of stays for a person, and the village's own name is never its own landmark (the Versova metro station does not beat the Versova circle). **Last resort, for anything the gazetteer does not hold** (a hotel, a building, a street, a peak, another state — "Mount Everest", "Regency Hotel, Andheri East", "Radhanagar Beach, Andaman"): the free OSM geocoders (Photon → Nominatim, cache first) pin a venue or a street at 300–500 m Medium, or a town/neighbourhood at 1.5 km Low; the pin names the service and says when it is outside the mapped districts.
- **The tricky rule:** "behind"/"opposite" aren't used for geometry; the circle carries that uncertainty. A long bridge is sampled every 150 m, so "near the bridge" measures to its nearest part. The case goes to the desk of the district **where the place is**, not where it was typed. A landmark named by the caller is first looked up under its extracted type; if no mapped name matches there, the name is looked for across the worship family (`temple`, `mosque`, `church`, `gurdwara`, `place_of_worship`) — the contract lets the model and the rules use any of those words for one feature, and a name that matches the map wins over the type guess (without it, two reports of one family can land on two different temples).
- **How to show it:** the demo line's circle; the seeded Low and needs-location cases; `rakshasetu.config.yaml` → `location.radius_m` ("it's our constant for that method, in this file").

- **Mumbai (m87):** the gazetteer scan tries two-word names before one-word names and stops at the first name said that matches ("Sion Koliwada" before "Koliwada"); a Devanagari word is also tried without a Marathi case ending (ला, त, मध्ये…). Neighbourhoods mapped as East and West halves get a derived joined place for callers who say just "Kurla".
